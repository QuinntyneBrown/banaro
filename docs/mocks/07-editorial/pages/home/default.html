The builders of Leslieville

On a few quiet blocks east of the Don, a food-bank scheduler, a service designer and a community lead are finding out what happens when your collaborators live a short walk away.

By the Banaro editors · 6 min read

Amara Osei started Harvest at her kitchen table in Leslieville. The idea fits on an index card: help GTA food banks schedule their volunteers without a spreadsheet, a group chat and a prayer. Six food banks now run their shifts on the beta. What Amara does not have yet is a technical co-founder.

That is the gap Banaro was built for. Search the directory from Leslieville and the city shrinks quickly. Esther Nguyen, a product designer who runs service-design workshops, lives 1.4 km away in Riverdale and is open to co-founding. Naomi Fraser, a community lead on the Danforth, is 2.1 km away and spends her week on events and partnerships.

A match, then a breakfast

The best match for Amara’s search lives further out. Daniel Reyes, a full-stack engineer in Mississauga, has ten years of Laravel behind him and builds Psalter, a scripture-memory app with 1,900 beta users, after hours. He scores 94% for what Harvest needs. Distance matters less once you have met in person.

Meeting in person is the point of the calendar. The Builders’ Prayer Breakfast gathers at St. Matthew’s Hall in Leslieville on Saturday 17 October at 8:00 am; 22 people are going. Two days earlier, Fall Demo Night fills the Centre for Social Innovation on Spadina with six demos and 64 builders, and 16 spots are still open.

Neighbours first

Banaro counts 1,284 builders across Toronto and the GTA, 312 projects and 96 co-founder matches so far. Few of them planned to start something with a neighbour. They joined to find one person with the skill they were missing, and found that person a streetcar ride away.

If you are building something that matters, the people who can help may already live on your street. Start with the directory; then come to breakfast.
