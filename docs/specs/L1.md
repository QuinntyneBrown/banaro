# L1 - High-Level Requirements

Banaro connects Christian product builders (founders, engineers, designers and product managers) in
Toronto and the Greater Toronto Area. These requirements describe what the system must do. Detailed,
testable behavior is in [L2.md](L2.md).

## L1-001: Accounts and authentication
The system must let a person join Banaro, verify their e-mail address, sign in, sign out, and recover
a forgotten password, and must keep their session secure and recoverable when it expires.

## L1-002: Builder profiles and onboarding
The system must let each builder present themselves with a profile (role, neighbourhood, skills,
experience, bio, photo, what they are building, and what they are open to), guide new builders through
onboarding, and let them edit their profile at any time.

## L1-003: Builder directory
The system must let people find local builders by skill, role, "open to" intent, neighbourhood and
distance, with search, sorting and paging, and must present each builder's profile to other members.

## L1-004: Project showcase
The system must let builders share a product in progress, list what stage it is at and what help it
needs, and let members browse and find projects.

## L1-005: Feedback and collaboration on projects
The system must let members give feedback on a project and offer to help with it, and must let the
project owner see and respond to that input.

## L1-006: Meetups and events
The system must let members discover local gatherings (demo nights, prayer breakfasts, workshops),
RSVP, join a waitlist when an event is full, and cancel an RSVP.

## L1-007: Co-founder and collaborator matching
The system must let a builder state who they are looking for (co-founder, advisor or contributor) and
must suggest nearby, compatible builders on a regular schedule, which the builder can review, act on,
pass on, or pause.

## L1-008: Messaging
The system must let members introduce themselves to one another and hold one-to-one conversations
about collaboration.

## L1-009: Notifications
The system must tell members about activity that concerns them (matches, messages, RSVPs, feedback)
in the app and by e-mail, and let members control what they receive.

## L1-010: Trust and safety
The system must give members the means to report a builder or content, block a builder, and read the
community's code of conduct, and must give administrators the means to act on reports.

## L1-011: Settings, privacy and account control
The system must let members manage their account settings, control who can see which parts of their
profile, control e-mail preferences, and permanently delete their account and data.

## L1-012: Public and informational pages
The system must provide a public home page and informational pages (about, code of conduct, privacy,
contact) so that visitors understand Banaro and can reach its team before signing up.

## L1-013: Resilience and system states
The system must present clear, recoverable states for loading, empty, error, forbidden, not-found,
offline and maintenance conditions, and must never leave a person without a way forward.

## L1-014: Security and data protection
The system must protect members' accounts and personal data, confine each member to their own data,
validate all input, and defend against the OWASP Top 10.

## L1-015: Performance
The system must respond quickly and render pages quickly under realistic load, and must not
degrade through layout shift or unbounded payloads.

## L1-016: Accessibility, responsiveness and theming
The system must be usable by people with disabilities (WCAG 2.2 AA), work on every viewport from
mobile to large desktop, and offer light and dark themes, matching the design reference in
`docs/mocks/`.

## L1-017: Localization and formatting
The system must present dates, times, distances and text consistently for a Toronto audience in
Canadian English, and must serve text from translation catalogues so further locales can be added.

## L1-018: Operability
The system must be observable, deployable without downtime, and safe to operate: explicit database
migrations, idempotent seed data, health endpoints, structured logs and rate limits.
